Summary of what i presented in the meeting:

We followed the Ensemble Distribution Distillation paper (arXiv:1905.00076).

  1. Teacher training: Trained an ensemble of models independently using standard cross-entropy loss.
  2. Distillation (EnD²): Built a transfer dataset by passing each training input through all teacher members to collect their categorical predictions. The student is trained on these inputs, learning to fit a Dirichlet distribution over the teacher categoricals via an NLL loss. Up to this point, our results matched the paper.
  3. OOD extension (our attempt): We tried explicitly training the student on OOD samples using a reverse-KL loss toward a uniform (flat) target Dirichlet. This did not improve OOD detection, it degraded in-domain classification performance instead.
  4. What the paper actually does differently: Rather than using a fixed flat target for OOD, the paper first passes the OOD samples through the (ID-trained) teacher ensemble as well (creates the transfer dataset for training OOD samples ), obtaining diverse teacher categoricals on OOD inputs (since the teacher wasn't trained on OOD data, its members naturally disagree there). The student then learns a Dirichlet from these diverse OOD categoricals, the flatness emerges from teacher disagreement, rather than being imposed as a fixed uniform target.

Next step: Re-run the OOD experiment following the paper's actual approach — pass OOD samples through the ID-trained teacher ensemble to get diverse teacher categoricals (instead of a fixed flat target). Also test the noise-as-proxy-OOD setup, where noise-perturbed ID samples (rather than a separate OOD dataset) are passed through the teacher, and check whether this noise signal improves OOD detection.

Hello sir, I went through the literature for the noise addition technique we are using in the code. I found that two standard papers are doing something very similar.

First is the BERT paper (Devlin et al., 2019), in their masked language model pretraining they select 15% of tokens and out of those 10% are replaced with a completely random vocabulary token. This is exactly what our code is doing — picking random positions and replacing them with random tokens from the vocab.

Second is the BART paper (Lewis et al., 2020), they introduce token shuffling as one of their noise strategies where they randomly shuffle the order of tokens in the input as a corruption technique. Our code does a softer version of this — instead of shuffling the whole sequence we shuffle within a small local window of 8 tokens around the selected position.

  1. The teacher (Bayesian Prompt Ensemble) gives us a set of probability distributions + weights
  2. One option would be to average these distribution and get the expected Categorical distribution for which we could use equation (6) from [1] and then proceed using equation (9) form [1]
  3. Alternatively (what we would like to start with) is Ensemble Distillation: we consider each of the probability distributions from the teacher as a “data point” to which we fit MLE of the student’s Dirichlet parameters \alpha: eq (8) in [2]
  4. Eventually, we can also rescale \alpha-s of the student based on a separate criterion.
  5. \alpha-s are softplus values of the student’s network logits, which are trained implicitly e.g. when training parameters of the student’s network
  6. Explicitly we train the student’s network parameters using LoRA
  7. We want to also train the last layer in the student network as the meaning of the logits changes from softmax in the pretrained Categorical likelihood to softplus of the Dirichlet likelihood
  8. When distilling we can have additional MAP term for the objective for the student network. MAP terms relies on a Categorical likelihood whereas our student uses the Dirichlet likelihood. There are two ways of getting a  Categorical likelihood from the Dirichlet likelihood:
  • use the expected Categorical distribution with probabilities p_k = \alpha_k / \alpha_0 - this option should be more stable during training (preferred)
  • sample Categorical distribution probabilities from Dirichlet e.g. p ~ Dirichlet(\alpha) - this option is more flexible.
